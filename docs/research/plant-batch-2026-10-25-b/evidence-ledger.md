## Access and evidence limits

Full abstracts and verified MED metadata are retained in numbered JSON files. Full open XML was accessed for PMID 34927694, 30764582, 36051888 and 41890334. EMA adopted monographs were fully downloaded and extracted for Linum (2015), Aesculus (2020) and Pelargonium (2024 revision 2); use these over stale public summaries, especially Pelargonium pediatric cutoffs, adverse-event frequency and liver warnings. The EDA roselle 2025-edition monograph was fully downloaded and read (last revision 2023). NCCIH flax and horse-chestnut complete HTML safety pages were read.

Europe PMC full-text XML requests for PMC11835051 (Pelargonium Cochrane), PMC7144685 (horse-chestnut Cochrane) and PMC3996458 (flax PK) returned server errors; full abstracts remain available. Rosehip papers were abstract-only; detailed safety and proprietary preparation data remain limited. PMID 34119421 has an internally inconsistent sample-size statement; no precise n/effect claim from it was carried into proposals.

## Scientific QA required

1. Confirm conservative per-indication grades, especially Level U for firm Pelargonium efficacy despite multiple reviews and Level B for manufacturer-supported rosehip trials.
2. Human compound-specific PK is available for roselle anthocyanins and flax lignans; do not generalize these to whole plants or all products.
3. Horse chestnut raw materials are toxic; standardized seed-extract results cannot justify oral use of raw seeds, bark, flowers or leaves.
4. Roselle literature uses synonym Hibiscus sabdariffa; maintain discoverability under the accepted name confirmed by taxonomy agent.
5. Rosehip product-specific powder cannot generalize to oils, teas or multi-species blends.
6. Entire batch remains DRAFT pending manual review.
