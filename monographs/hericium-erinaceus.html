Hericium erinaceus

Lion's Mane / Pom Pom Mushroom

For educational and scientific research only. Not medical advice. Independently verify claims against the cited primary sources. Consult a qualified healthcare professional before using any plant or fungal preparation.

Research areas: Neurological & Cognitive Research, Cognitive Function Research

Overview · Clinical data and safety · Sources

Research overview

Historical use

Used in Traditional Chinese and Japanese medicine for centuries primarily to treat gastrointestinal disorders (gastric ulcers, chronic gastritis) and as a general tonic. Known in Japan as 'Yamabushitake'. Referenced in historical texts as a food and medicine for digestive health.

Modern research context

Clinically studied for its nerve growth factor (NGF)-stimulating properties. A 2009 double-blind RCT demonstrated significant improvement in mild cognitive impairment after 16 weeks of supplementation [1]. A 4-week pilot study documented reduction in depression and anxiety scores [4]. Neuroprotective mechanisms include antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity mediated by hericenones and erinacines [2][3]. Evidence supports investigation as a neuroprotective agent in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease models [2][3].

Adverse effects

Generally well-tolerated. Reported adverse effects include mild GI discomfort (nausea, bloating), and rare allergic reactions including contact dermatitis and respiratory allergy in sensitised individuals.

Contraindications

Known allergy to Hericium species or other mushrooms. Caution with anticoagulants due to potential platelet inhibition. Caution in autoimmune conditions given immunomodulatory properties.

Clinical and phytochemical data

Used part

Fruiting body (hericenones) and mycelium (erinacines)

Primary active compounds

  • Hericenones A–H (cyathane diterpenoids, fruiting body)

  • Erinacines A–K (cyathane diterpenoids, mycelium)

  • Beta-glucans

  • Ergosterol

Mechanism of action

Hericenones and erinacines stimulate de novo synthesis and secretion of NGF (nerve growth factor) in astrocytes, promoting neuronal survival and axonal growth. Erinacines cross the blood-brain barrier (demonstrated in rodent studies) via passive diffusion. Anti-neuroinflammatory effects via inhibition of MAPK and NF-κB pathways. Promotes hippocampal neurogenesis via TrkA/Erk1/2 signalling.

Pharmacokinetics

Absorption

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Erinacine A crosses the blood-brain barrier in rodents; human CNS penetration not directly measured. Hericenone bioavailability in humans is not well characterised.

Distribution

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Erinacine A distributes into CNS tissue in rodent models, including the hippocampus and cortex. Hericenones are lipophilic and expected to distribute into lipid-rich tissues. No human plasma protein binding or tissue distribution data are available.

Metabolism

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Limited human data. Hepatic phase I/II metabolism assumed based on compound structure.

Excretion

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Limited human data. Presumed renal and biliary excretion of metabolites.

Safety and interactions

Drug interactions

Lack of clinical studies prevents interaction grading. Unknown safety profile with concomitant drug use.

Toxicity

LD50 >5 g/kg (oral, mice). No organ toxicity identified in 28-day subacute studies in rodents. Human clinical trials up to 3 g/day for 16 weeks showed no significant adverse effects.

Special precautions

Pregnancy

Insufficient human clinical data. No animal teratogenicity studies identified. Avoid due to lack of safety evidence.

Lactation

Excretion in human breast milk unknown. Avoid during breastfeeding.

Hepatic impairment

No hepatotoxicity reported in literature. No dose adjustment studies available; use with standard monitoring.

Renal impairment

No specific contraindication identified. Limited data in renal impairment populations.
