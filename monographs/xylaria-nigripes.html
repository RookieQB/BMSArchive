Xylaria nigripes

Wu Ling Shen

Overview · Clinical data and safety · Sources

Research overview

Historical use

Known in Traditional Chinese Medicine as 'Wu Ling Shen' (the fungus found inside the chambers of ancient termite mounds). Used historically for insomnia, anxiety, and to calm the mind. Referenced in TCM pharmacopeias as a sedative tonic. Grows inside the nests of Odontotermes formosanus (Formosan subterranean termite) in a unique symbiotic relationship.

Modern research context

Studied primarily for sedative, anxiolytic, and neuroprotective properties; metabolites, biological activities, and clinical applications have been comprehensively reviewed [1]. A multicenter randomised controlled trial documented significant antidepressant effects in epilepsy patients receiving X. nigripes extract alongside standard therapy [2]. Anti-inflammatory polysaccharide fractions exhibit synergistic activity in vitro [3]. Novel resorcinol derivatives [4] and sesquiterpenoids [5] with neuroprotective activities have been isolated from the fruiting body. Commercial preparations are marketed in Taiwan and China for insomnia and anxiety, though broader clinical data remain limited [1].

Adverse effects

Insufficient human clinical data. Potential sedation and CNS depression. No significant toxicity reported in preclinical studies.

Contraindications

CNS depressants (benzodiazepines, alcohol, barbiturates, opioids) — potential additive sedation. Caution when operating machinery. Insufficient data for comprehensive contraindication profile.

Clinical and phytochemical data

Used part

Sclerotium found inside termite mounds

Mechanism of action

Positive allosteric modulation of GABA-A receptor (benzodiazepine-like effect), prolonging inhibitory chloride channel opening. 5-HT reuptake inhibition proposed in animal models. Anti-inflammatory effects via NF-κB inhibition and reduction of IL-6, TNF-α. Adenosine receptor modulation may contribute to anxiolytic and sleep-promoting effects.

Safety and interactions

Drug interactions

CNS depressants — additive sedative effect (theoretical, based on GABA-A mechanism). Alcohol — potential potentiation of CNS depression. No drug interaction studies in humans.

Toxicity

LD50 not established for human use. Preclinical animal studies show no significant organ toxicity at doses up to 5 g/kg. Human safety data absent.

Special precautions

Pregnancy

Insufficient data. Avoid during pregnancy.

Lactation

Insufficient data. Avoid during breastfeeding.

Hepatic impairment

No clinical data are available in hepatic impairment. No hepatotoxicity has been identified in preclinical studies. The GABA-A modulating activity of nigrigenin may theoretically be potentiated in hepatic encephalopathy due to altered GABAergic tone; use with caution and standard monitoring.

Renal impairment

No clinical data are available in renal impairment. No specific nephrotoxicity has been identified in preclinical studies. Standard monitoring is recommended; renal excretion pathway for nigrigenin metabolites has not been characterised.
