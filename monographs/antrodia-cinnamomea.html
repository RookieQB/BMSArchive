Antrodia cinnamomea

Niu Zhang Zhi

Research areas: Hepatic / Liver Mechanisms, Cellular & Immune Studies, Inflammatory Pathways

Overview · Clinical data and safety · Sources

Research overview

Historical use

Endemic to Taiwan, where it grows exclusively on the inner cavity of Cinnamomum kanehirai (Taiwanese sassafras). Known locally as 'Zhang Zhi' or 'Niu Zhang Zhi'. Used in Taiwanese indigenous medicine and folk medicine for centuries as a treatment for liver disease, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, and as a hangover remedy. Historically one of the most expensive medicinal fungi in Taiwan.

Modern research context

Hepatoprotective and anti-tumour properties are the primary research focus. Clinical evidence supports hepatoprotective activity in patients with non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) [1] and in sub-health adults [2]. Polysaccharide intervention modulates clinical symptoms likely via gut microbiome regulation [3]. Anti-proliferative activity against prostate cancer via immune modulation has been documented [4]. Hepatoprotective mechanisms are further supported by preclinical data showing reduction of hepatotoxicity in rodent models [5]. Antroquinonol, a unique ubiquinone derivative, has entered Phase II clinical trials in Taiwan.

Adverse effects

Generally well tolerated. Mild GI symptoms. Rare hypersensitivity. Potential QT interval prolongation with high doses (preclinical).

Contraindications

Hepatotoxic drugs — monitor liver function. Anticoagulants — theoretical additive effect. Drugs affecting QT interval — use with caution (preclinical signal).

Clinical and phytochemical data

Used part

Fruiting body and mycelium

Primary active compounds

  • Antroquinonol (4-hydroxy-2,3-dimethoxy-6-methyl-cyclohex-2-enone derivative)

  • Triterpenoids (antcin A, B, C, K)

  • Polysaccharides

  • Maleic/succinic acid derivatives

  • Ergosterol peroxide

Mechanism of action

Antroquinonol inhibits Ras/Akt/mTOR signalling, inducing tumour cell autophagy and apoptosis; also inhibits HIF-1α, reducing hypoxia-driven VEGF expression (anti-angiogenic). Triterpenoids inhibit NF-κB pathway, reducing IL-6, TNF-α, and COX-2. Hepatoprotective via Nrf2/HO-1 upregulation and suppression of CYP2E1-mediated oxidative stress.

Pharmacokinetics

Absorption

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Intestinal absorption of 14 ergostane and lanostane triterpenoids from Antrodia cinnamomea was characterised using a bidirectional Caco-2 cell monolayer model; most triterpenoids showed low-to-moderate intestinal permeability, consistent with their high lipophilicity [1]. No human oral bioavailability data are available. Antroquinonol bioavailability ~20% in rodents. Triterpenoid absorption limited; improved by lipid-based formulation. Human PK data emerging from Phase I/II trials.

Distribution

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Antroquinonol and ubiquinone derivatives are highly lipophilic; distribute to tumour vasculature, liver, and adipose tissue. Significant hepatic first-pass metabolism expected. Half-life of antroquinonol estimated at ~4–6 hours in preclinical models. No formal human PK distribution data available.

Metabolism

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Hepatic CYP3A4-mediated metabolism of triterpenoids. Antroquinonol undergoes phase II conjugation.

Excretion

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Biliary/fecal for triterpenoids; renal for water-soluble metabolites.

Safety and interactions

Drug interactions

Lack of clinical studies prevents interaction grading. Unknown safety profile with concomitant drug use.

Toxicity

LD50 >5 g/kg for whole extract (rodents). Antroquinonol demonstrated an acceptable tolerability profile in Phase I clinical evaluation. QT prolongation was observed at suprapharmacological doses in preclinical studies — clinical significance at therapeutic doses is unclear; long-term safety data are limited to early-phase clinical experience.

Special precautions

Pregnancy

Insufficient clinical data. Potent bioactivity warrants avoidance during pregnancy.

Lactation

No data on breast milk excretion. Avoid during breastfeeding.

Hepatic impairment

Paradox: hepatoprotective in documented trials, but CYP metabolism may be impaired; monitor carefully and start at low doses.

Renal impairment

No specific data. Standard monitoring recommended.
