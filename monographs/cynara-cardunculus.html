Plant · Scientific monograph

Cynara cardunculus

Artichoke

For educational and scientific research only. Not medical advice. Independently verify claims against the cited primary sources. Consult a qualified healthcare professional before using any plant or fungal preparation.

Research areas: Digestive health, Lipid metabolism

Overview · Clinical data and safety · Sources

Research overview

Historical use

Artichoke leaf has documented traditional use for fullness, bloating and flatulence; EMA categorizes this digestive indication as traditional use [4].

Modern research context

Artichoke leaf extracts have been investigated for functional dyspepsia and lipid biomarkers [1][2][3]. In a 6-week RCT (247 enrolled), a commercial leaf extract improved symptom scores versus placebo [1]. Meta-analyses reported lipid changes across varying products; cardiovascular event prevention is unproven [2][3]. BMS Level B provisional for the specific dyspepsia preparation and lipid markers, with full-text bias review pending; EMA traditional-use status remains distinct [4].

Adverse effects

Mild diarrhea, abdominal spasm, nausea, heartburn and allergic reactions are reported; frequencies are unknown [4].

Contraindications

EMA lists Asteraceae hypersensitivity, bile-duct obstruction, cholangitis, liver disease, gallstones and other biliary disorders requiring medical advice. Use below age 12 is not established [4]. Persistent digestive symptoms beyond two weeks warrant medical advice [4].

Clinical and phytochemical data

Used part

Leaf extract of cultivated artichoke, reported in literature as Cynara scolymus; evidence must not be transferred to all C. cardunculus varieties or edible hearts. EMA covers specified leaf preparations [1][4].

Primary active compounds

  • Commercial leaf-extract mixture; the retrieved trial abstract does not identify constituent concentrations or clinical standardization, so no individual active compound is assigned [1].

Mechanism of action

Digestive symptom modulation and lipid metabolism are studied outcomes; molecular causation is not established by the retrieved human trials. No specific molecular target is assigned here [1][2][3].

Pharmacokinetics

Absorption

Human absorption of the trial extract is not characterized in the retrieved studies; no bioavailability estimate assigned [1][4].

Distribution

Human tissue distribution is not characterized in the retrieved sources [1][4].

Metabolism

Human metabolism of the whole leaf extract is not characterized in this extraction; no CYP claim assigned [4].

Excretion

No human elimination route or half-life established for the whole extract in the retrieved sources [4].

Safety and interactions

Drug interactions

EMA reports no interactions; formal interaction data remain limited. This does not establish compatibility with all medicines [4].

Toxicity

Long-term human toxicity is not established by short trials. EMA notes inadequate reproductive, genotoxicity and carcinogenicity testing; no quantified human safe maximum is assigned [1][4].

Special precautions

Pregnancy

Safety unestablished; EMA does not recommend medicinal use during pregnancy [4].

Lactation

Safety unestablished; EMA does not recommend medicinal use during lactation [4].

Hepatic impairment

Liver disease and biliary disease are listed contraindications in EMA; experimental liver research does not override this [4].

Renal impairment

No renal dose-adjustment evidence identified; renal-impairment safety remains insufficiently characterized [4].
