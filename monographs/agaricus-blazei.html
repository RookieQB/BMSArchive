Agaricus blazei

Royal Sun Agaricus / ABM

Research areas: Immunological Research, Glucose & Metabolic Research, Cellular & Immune Studies

Overview · Clinical data and safety · Sources

Research overview

Historical use

Native to the Piedade region of Brazil, where it is known as 'Cogumelo do Sol' (mushroom of the sun) or 'Cogumelo de Deus' (mushroom of God). Introduced to Japan in the 1960s, where it became popular after researchers noted low rates of cancer and infections in the Brazilian community that consumed it regularly.

Modern research context

Studied primarily for immunomodulation and anti-tumour activity [1][4]. A Norwegian randomised clinical trial with AndoSan preparation demonstrated reduced inflammatory cytokine markers (TNF-α, IL-6) in blood donors [2]. Reduced inflammatory mediators were also observed in elderly women in a separate randomised trial [3]. Chemical composition analysis confirms beta-glucans and ergosterol as the primary bioactives driving immunomodulatory effects [4][5].

Adverse effects

GI upset (nausea, diarrhoea). Rare hepatotoxicity reported (case reports, likely idiosyncratic). Eosinophilia reported in one RCT. Hypoglycaemia risk.

Contraindications

Anticoagulants — potential additive effect. Antidiabetic drugs — additive hypoglycaemia risk. Pre-existing liver disease — use with caution given case reports of hepatotoxicity.

Clinical and phytochemical data

Used part

Fruiting body

Primary active compounds

  • Beta-1,3/1,6-D-glucans (high content, up to 40% dry weight)

  • Polysaccharides (AbPS)

  • Ergosterol

  • Blazein (steroid compound)

  • Agaritine (hydrazine derivative — potentially genotoxic)

Mechanism of action

Beta-glucans activate macrophages and NK cells via Dectin-1; upregulate Th1 cytokines (IL-12, IFN-γ, TNF-α). Anti-tumour mechanisms include direct cytotoxicity via induction of apoptosis (caspase-3 pathway) and anti-angiogenic effects. Antidiabetic mechanism involves PPAR-γ activation and improved insulin receptor signalling. Agaritine is a potentially carcinogenic hydrazine compound degraded by cooking.

Pharmacokinetics

Absorption

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Beta-glucan fractions partially absorbed; primarily active at GALT. Detailed human PK data not available.

Distribution

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Beta-glucan fractions concentrate at gut-associated lymphoid tissue (GALT), spleen, and mesenteric lymph nodes; limited systemic distribution. Blazein (steroid compound) distributes broadly due to lipophilicity; plasma protein binding not established in humans. No quantitative human tissue distribution studies available.

Metabolism

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Hepatic metabolism of absorbed fractions. Agaritine hydrolysed by gut bacteria and enzymes; cooking significantly reduces agaritine content.

Excretion

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Primarily fecal for polysaccharides; renal for smaller metabolites.

Safety and interactions

Drug interactions

Lack of clinical studies prevents interaction grading. Unknown safety profile with concomitant drug use.

Toxicity

LD50 >5 g/kg (oral, rodents). Rare cases of hepatotoxicity in humans (likely idiosyncratic). Agaritine content: raw mushroom contains up to 500 mg/kg; cooking, drying, or storage reduces this substantially. Long-term human clinical data on agaritine exposure are insufficient to fully characterise the risk.

Special precautions

Pregnancy

Insufficient safety data. Presence of agaritine (potentially genotoxic hydrazine derivative) warrants avoidance during pregnancy.

Lactation

Insufficient data. Avoid concentrated supplemental preparations during breastfeeding.

Hepatic impairment

Case reports of hepatotoxicity exist; avoid in patients with hepatic disease or monitor liver enzymes carefully.

Renal impairment

No specific data available. Standard monitoring recommended.
