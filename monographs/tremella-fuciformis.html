Tremella fuciformis

Snow Fungus / Silver Ear Mushroom

Research areas: Neurological & Cognitive Research, Dermatological Research, Immunological Research

Overview · Clinical data and safety · Sources

Research overview

Historical use

Used in Traditional Chinese Medicine and as a prized culinary delicacy for over 2,000 years. Known in China as 'Bai Mu Er' (white wood ear). Historically associated with female beauty and longevity — legend holds that Yang Guifei, the Tang Dynasty imperial consort famed for her beauty, used it daily. Used medicinally for lung conditions, dry cough, and as a yin tonic.

Modern research context

Tremella polysaccharide (TP) is studied for neuroprotective effects (NGF-like activity), skin hydration, and immunomodulation via multiple molecular mechanisms [1]. The high water-retention capacity of TP — comparable to hyaluronic acid — underpins its extensive use in cosmetic dermatology [2]. A clinical trial demonstrated improvements in glycated haemoglobin (HbA1c) and waist circumference in overweight subjects following T. fuciformis supplementation [3]. Anti-proliferative activity against EBV-associated gastric cancer has been documented via ferroptosis induction [4]. Anti-inflammatory properties relevant to atopic dermatitis are under preclinical investigation [5].

Adverse effects

Very well tolerated. Rare allergic reactions. No significant adverse effects documented in available clinical literature.

Contraindications

No absolute contraindications established. Caution with immunosuppressants due to immunostimulant properties.

Mechanism of action

Tremella polysaccharides stimulate NGF secretion in astrocytes (similar mechanism to Lion's Mane), promoting neuronal differentiation and survival. Anti-inflammatory effects via inhibition of NF-κB and COX-2. Skin-hydrating effects attributed to polysaccharide hygroscopic properties and upregulation of hyaluronic acid synthase (HAS2). Immunomodulation via TLR-4 activation.

Pharmacokinetics

Absorption

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Oral bioavailability of TP is low due to high molecular weight. Some fractions absorbed in the GI tract; bioactive effects also mediated at mucosal level. Human PK data very limited.

Distribution

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — High-molecular-weight polysaccharides remain largely confined to the GI tract and GALT; smaller oligosaccharide fragments may enter systemic circulation via lymphatic uptake. Skin distribution is suggested by clinical hydration data, consistent with promotion of dermal hyaluronic acid synthesis. No formal human PK distribution studies.

Metabolism

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Partially fermented by gut microbiota to SCFAs. Absorbed fractions undergo hepatic metabolism.

Excretion

No human pharmacokinetic data are available for this species. The following is derived from preclinical animal studies and in vitro data only, and may not reflect human pharmacology. — Primarily fecal; renal for absorbed small molecular weight fragments.

Safety and interactions

Drug interactions

Lack of clinical studies prevents interaction grading. Unknown safety profile with concomitant drug use.

Toxicity

LD50 >10 g/kg (oral, mice). No organ toxicity in preclinical studies. Human clinical data very limited but no safety signals identified.

Special precautions

Pregnancy

Insufficient human clinical data. Dietary consumption likely safe; concentrated supplements — avoid due to lack of evidence.

Lactation

No data on breast milk excretion. Dietary use likely acceptable; supplements — insufficient data.

Hepatic impairment

No hepatotoxicity documented. Standard precautions apply.

Renal impairment

No specific risk identified. No dose adjustment data.
